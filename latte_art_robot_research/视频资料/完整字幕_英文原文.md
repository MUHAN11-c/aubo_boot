## Part 1: 牛奶科学 (Milk Science) — 00:00 ~ 05:00

> In this video we're going to learn some basic barista science about milk, how to heat and texture milk to form a delicious base for drinks, and how to pour it to make drinks look pretty. Before we start steaming and pouring milk, it is important to learn some basic milk chemistry and the rules that govern the way we treat milk when we steam it.

> As far as we're concerned as baristas, milk consists of three main components: **sugars, proteins, and fats**. Let's discuss each one and the role it plays in both preparation and taste.

> Let's start with sugars. **The main sugar in milk is lactose** and is actually very sweet. This explains why well heated and textured cappuccinos and lattes often don't need extra sugar to taste sweet. This plays a role in the temperature of our beverages because we want to taste every bit of sweetness we can pull out of the milk.

> Imagine a fresh piping hot piece of pizza. If you put it in your mouth immediately, all you taste is heat and pain. If you let that same pizza cool a little, though you can taste everything that's delicious and wonderful about melted cheese and saucy bread. By the same logic then **you don't want your lattes to be too hot** to taste all that lovely lactose.

> Next up: our **proteins**. Proteins begin as coiled balls in cold milk and through the addition of heat uncoil to expose a hydrophilic (or water-loving) end and hydrophobic (or water-hating) end. When we pull air into the milk with the steam wand the hydrophobic ends of the proteins will hide from the water in the milk inside the air bubble causing the hydrophilic ends to form a barrier around the bubble keeping its shape. This is known as a **surfactant**. The surfactant qualities of proteins are what holds the air bubbles together to create milk foam and will keep its shape until about **160°F (71°C)**, at which point the proteins will fall apart or **denature**. This causes the air bubbles the proteins are holding to pop and the milk foam to deteriorate-- yet another reason we don't want to steam too hot.

> Our third and final milk component is **fat**. Fats affect the thickness of the milk and quality and longevity of the foam. **The more fat present in the milk, the thicker the liquid and the longer the foam will last.** Imagine milk foam as a series of bubbles stacked on top of one another. In between each one of these bubbles is a tiny space that will let liquid fall through it. The more fat present in the milk, the thicker that liquid will be and the longer it will take to fall between the gaps and the bubbles, creating a stiff dry layer of foam on top of liquid milk.

> The other effect fats have on the steaming process is that it gives the hydrophobic parts of proteins another option to hide inside. **The less fat present in milk, the more the proteins will focus on the air we inject, creating larger amounts of foam.**

### 四个关键温度

> Now that we've talked about the three main components in milk, let's discuss **four key temperatures** and how they affect each component.

> **40°F (4°C)** — right out of the fridge. At this point the sugars are too cold to really taste, the proteins are in coiled balls, and the fats are in solid globules.

> **100°F (37°C)** — about people temperature. Here the sugars are just starting to get yummy, the proteins are uncoiling, and the fats are melting into oils. Because the fats are going from a solid to a liquid, it gives the proteins another option of something to grab on to, disrupting foam formation. **It's for this reason that we want to stop adding air when the milk reaches 100°F (37°C)**, when it's just starting to feel warm.

> **140°F (60°C)** — the sugars are in full effect, the proteins are uncoiled, and all the fats have turned to oil. **This is our ideal tasting temperature, so stop the steaming process once the milk is 140°F (60°C) or too hot to touch.**

> **160°F (71°C)** — the sugars are too hot to taste, the proteins are denaturing. At this point, not only is the milk not sweet and the foam starting to fail, but **the taste of denatured protein is bitter and eggy**--a poor complement to espresso.

## Part 2: 打奶泡技术 (Steaming) — 05:00 ~ 13:15

### 奶量计算

> The first step is steaming perfectly textured milk for an espresso beverage is to determine the right amount of cold milk to pour into the appropriately sized pitcher for that drink. The final volume of milk in the pitcher after steaming should equal the volume of the drink you're about to pour, and with about an ounce (30mL) of espresso already taking up room in the cup, that should leave you with an ounce of milk (30mL) left over in the pitcher when you're through pouring.

> **For lattes we want 25% aeration and for cappuccinos, 50%.**

### 各杯型奶量对照表

| 饮品 | 杯量 | 冷牛奶量 | 奶缸大小 | 打后位置 |
|------|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Cappuccino (堂食) | 6 oz (178mL) | 3 oz (88mL) | 标准 | 注气后到壶嘴底 |
| Cappuccino (外带) | 8 oz (236mL) | 4 oz (118mL) | 标准 | 注气后到壶嘴一半 |
| Latte (外带) | 8 oz (236mL) | 6 oz (178mL) | 12 oz (355mL) | 注气后到壶嘴一半 |
| Latte | 12 oz (355mL) | 9 oz (266mL) | 20 oz (590mL) | 注气后到壶嘴 1/4 |
| Latte | 16 oz (473mL) | 12 oz (355mL) | 20 oz (590mL) | 注气后到壶嘴 3/4 |
| Latte | 20 oz (590mL) | 16 oz (473mL) | 36 oz (~1L) | 注气后到壶嘴 1/4 |

### 打奶泡操作步骤

> **Step 1: 排冷凝水** — At the machine begin by opening the steam valve for at least one second to remove any condensation from inside the steam wand and prevent injecting your milk with water.

> **Step 2: 蒸汽棒角度** — Next position the steam wand at a **10 to 20 degree angle** straight out from the machine. This will position all the holes on the steam wand tip at a downward angle, allowing it to pull air into the milk instead of skipping it across the surface.

> **Step 3: 初始深度** — Next submerge the steam wand into the milk just to the point where the steam wand tip screws into the steam wand itself. This will allow you to open the steam valve all the way without immediately pulling in air.

> **Step 4: 倾斜奶缸** — Tilt the pitcher slightly to one side or the other allowing the angle of the steam wand to push the milk in a circular motion around the pitcher. This allows you to control the high water mark in the pitcher and inject air only when you choose to.

> **Step 5: 注入空气 (进气)** — In one motion open the steam valve all the way and lower the pitcher until you hear the steam wand start to pull air into the milk.

### 声音判断法

| 声音 | 含义 | 操作 |
|------|------|------|
| **持续撕纸声** (constant paper tearing) | 进气量正确 | 保持 |
| **间歇啾啾声** (intermittent chirp) | 进气不足 | 降低奶缸 |
| **电视雪花声** (TV static) | 进气太多 | 抬高奶缸 |

> **Step 6: 停止进气时机** — Continue to consistently add air until the side of the pitcher feels warm to the touch (100°F/37°C) or you have the appropriate amount of aeration, **whichever comes first**.

> **Step 7: 加热打绵** — To stop aerating, raise the pitcher just enough to resubmerge the holes in the tip of the steam wand. Hold this position allowing the steam wand to heat and swirl the milk in the pitcher until it is **140°F (60°C) or too hot to touch**.

> **Step 8: 关蒸汽** — Once the milk is hot enough turn off the steam valve in one motion, lower the pitcher to the counter, purge out any residual milk on the inside of the steam wand, and thoroughly clean the outside of the steam wand with a damp cloth.

### 收尾处理

> Even if everything goes perfectly there might be some small blemishes on the surface of the milk when you're through. So **tap the pitcher on a hard surface to pop any visible bubbles and swirl the milk for at least three seconds until it's glossy and shiny like wet paint or marshmallow fluff.**

### 进气不足 vs 进气过多

> **进气不足** (not enough air): The end result is aerated milk with a thin watery texture and bubbles that won't pop out when you tap the pitcher. The milk won't expand — you won't have enough steamed milk in the pitcher to fill the customer's cup.

> **进气过多** (too much air): The result is thick pillowy foam that stiffens quickly and is difficult to pour.

## Part 3: 拉花艺术 (Latte Art) — 13:15 ~ 28:56

### 3.1 拉花的物理原理

> A good way to think about latte art is like plating for a chef. You've taken your time to craft a delicious shot of espresso, and you've played by all the rules to create a perfectly textured milk base. **Latte art is your chance to grab the customer's attention and communicate to them that this drink is going to be amazing.**

### 3.2 变量一: 高度 (Height)

> Imagine a bubble of milk foam as a white ball we're dropping into a colored liquid. **If the ball is submerged in the liquid, it will be stained that color when it pops back up to the surface, whereas if the ball remains on the surface, it will stay white.** Dropping the ball from higher-up guarantees that it will submerge when it hits the liquid, whereas dropping it from lower will keep it on the surface.

三个关键高度:

| 高度 | 效果 | 用途 |
|------|------|------|
| **3 英寸 (7.6 cm) 以上** | 奶泡沉入 espresso 下方, 表面保持深棕色 | 融合阶段 |
| **约 1 英寸 (2.5 cm)** | 部分奶泡沉入, 部分浮面 → 浑浊米色 (beige) | **应避免!** |
| **1/4 英寸 (0.6 cm) 以内** | 奶泡停留在表面, 保持纯白色 | 成形阶段 (画图案) |

> The way to make sure your design is crisp white is to keep the spout of the pitcher **within a quarter inch (.6 cm)** of the liquid in the cup when pouring the design.

### 3.3 倾斜杯子的技巧

> In order to get as close as possible as soon as possible, **tilt the cup at a thirty to forty-five degree angle** and make sure the pitcher is touching the lip of the cup the entire time you're forming the design.

关键流程:
1. 杯子倾斜 45° (30-45° 都可以)
2. 从 3 英寸以上高度开始注入 (融合)
3. 液面到达杯口时 → 停止注入
4. 奶缸接触杯口 → 立即继续注入 (成形)
5. 缓慢回正杯子防止溢出

> A good rule of thumb: **if any beige appears in your design, that means the spout of the pitcher was not within a quarter inch (.6 cm) of the liquid in the cup.**

### 3.4 变量二: 流速 + 表面张力

> As we pour milk and foam into the cup, surface tension begins to build, starting with very low surface tension when the cup is empty and high surface tension once the cup is full.

> **The sooner you begin the design, the more the design can flow across the cup.** The later you begin the design, the smaller and more compressed it will be.

> Because surface tension is building as we fill the cup, it's important to **continue to increase the rate of pour from the pitcher to keep designs flowing.**

### 3.5 变量三: 奶缸位置 (Position)

核心概念 — "气球原理": 拉花图案像吹气球一样在奶缸前方形成, 往下流到底部, 再绕回来。

> The way to pour a **centered design** is to place the spout of the pitcher **slightly above the center**, allowing the balloon to fill up below the spout.

位置错误对照:

| 奶嘴位置 | 结果 |
|---------|------|
| 放在杯正中心 | 图案沉到杯底, 上半部空 |
| 放在杯顶部 (太高) | 图案往下流但不会绕回来 → 长条形 |
| 放在杯一侧 | 产生螺旋水流 → 一边大一边小 |
| **杯中心稍上方** ✓ | 图案居中, 均匀展开 |

### 3.6 基础图案一: 实心心形 (Solid Heart / Monk's Head)

> **So far in these examples we've been pouring a traditional cappuccino design referred to as a monk's head.** While a monk's head is perfectly fine in its own right, let's put a tail at the bottom to transform it into a solid heart shape.

心形步骤:
1. 按前面的步骤做出好的 Monk's Head
2. 当杯子快满时, 把奶缸抬高到 3 英寸 (7.6 cm) 标记 → 停止主动成形
3. 缓慢将奶流移到杯底 → 把圆形拉成心形的尖尾巴
4. 到达杯底时突然停止注入

> This technique is referred to as the **draw through**, and will finish most common latte art designs.

Draw-through 注意事项:
- 确保抬高到 3 英寸以上, 否则会在图案上拉出一条泡沫线
- **减小流速**, 缓慢移动
- 从图案中心穿过

### 3.7 进阶: 摆动纹理 (Rippled Heart)

> To add even layers to a design, maintain a consistent flow rate from the pitcher and **add a very slight back-and-forth motion with your wrist.** This will cause the milk to start to swing in a **pendulum motion** inside the pitcher.

> The goal is to have a **consistent even pendulum motion** from the milk in the pitcher and **avoid twitchiness** by keeping your flow rate consistent and your motions even.

摆动要点:
- 手腕做**非常轻微**的来回运动
- 牛奶在奶缸内形成**钟摆式运动**
- 奶缸实际移动幅度很小
- 流速要保持高且稳定
- 避免"抽搐式"抖动

### 3.8 郁金香 (Tulip)

> This is a great design for beginners because it gives you the chance to start and stop to correct any mistakes you've made throughout the pour.

郁金香步骤:
1. 在杯中心做一个**波纹心形** (rippled heart)
2. 心形顶部开始成形时 → **突然停止注入** (不做 draw-through)
3. 奶缸嘴放在心形交汇点**正上方**
4. 在波纹基底上注入一个**实心心形**
5. 如果杯子满了 → 抬高奶缸做 draw-through
6. 如果还有空间 → 重复加层

关键注意事项:
- 层与层之间**停顿越短越好** (牛奶和奶泡会分离)
- 如果图案偏了, 可以通过调整下一层的位置来纠正
- 可以停和开始任意多次, 加任意多层

### 3.9 两种基本形状理论

> **In latte art there are two main shapes we can form, and how we combine those shapes determines the design.**

| 形状 | 形成方式 | 结果 |
|------|---------|------|
| **心形 (Heart)** | 保持位置或**向前推** | 圆形卷曲 |
| **叶形 (Leaf)** | **向后拉**奶缸 | 线条堆叠 |

### 3.10 树叶 / Rosetta

> The rosetta is a much more difficult design to pour because it involves a lot of movement and doesn't give you much time to assess and adjust mid pour. **Don't be frustrated if this design takes a while to get down and remember not to practice on customers. A great-looking solid heart always looks better than a rickety rosetta.**

Rosetta 步骤:
1. 奶缸嘴放在杯中心**稍上方**
2. 轻轻左右摇晃 → 形成**均匀的钟摆运动**
3. 看到心形顶部开始成形时 (和郁金香一样)
4. **不要突然停止!** 而是 **缓慢将奶缸向后拉向杯顶**
5. 保持流速一致, 钟摆运动均匀
6. 奶缸嘴到达杯顶时 → 停住 → 让杯子注满形成实心心形
7. 杯子满后 → 轻轻做 **draw-through** 穿过中心收尾

> Make sure not to lessen your flow rate or make any jerky motions with the pitcher at this point as that will result in twitchy looking uneven leaves.

### 3.11 结语

> Now we've covered all the basic techniques and shapes to pour great latte art. Once you're comfortable with everything we've covered so far, feel free to get creative and combine these designs and techniques in different ways.

> **Remember though that with great latte art comes great responsibility, and when you pour something gorgeous for a customer you're promising them you've done all the work up until that point to make sure their drink is going to taste just as good as it looks.**
